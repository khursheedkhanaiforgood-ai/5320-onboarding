Guest Essentials + AirDefense
in XIQ Classic

Khursheed Khan  •  May 20, 2026  •  Two sprints · XIQ Classic only · Not available in EP1 yet

Both Guest Essentials and AirDefense Essentials are XIQ Classic only — they appear as "Planned" in the EP1 feature roadmap. This guide walks through both from scratch, with exact navigation paths, field names, and verification steps. Follow Sprint 1 (GE) fully before starting Sprint 2 (AD).

Prerequisite

Both services require an ExtremeCloud IQ Pilot license on your account. Log into XIQ Classic — not EP1. If your account shows only a Connect license, GE and AirDefense will not appear under Essentials.

Sprint 1 — Guest Essentials

Guest Essentials is ExtremeCloud IQ's guest lifecycle management service. It wraps your open/CWP SSID with a self-service registration portal, time-limited access, sponsor approval workflows, and analytics. It sits on top of the network policy you already built in XIQ — the AP still handles the wireless, GE handles the identity and portal layer.

Step 6 — Create Onboarding Rule (the SSID → Policy link)

The onboarding rule is what GE uses to match an incoming guest connection to a policy. It links: this SSID + this location → apply this onboarding policy. Without a rule, GE won't know what to do with guests connecting to KhKLab_Guest.

Configure → Onboarding → Rules → + (Add)
FieldValueNotes
Rule NameKhKLab_GuestRuleAny unique name
PolicyKhKLab_GuestOnboardThe policy you created in Step 3
NetworkKhKLab_GuestMust match the SSID broadcast name exactly
LocationKhursheedsLab (or Global)Match the location assigned to AP1 in XIQ
Precedence1Lower number = higher priority. Use 1 for your only rule.

Click SAVE.

Precedence logic

If multiple rules match (same SSID, different locations), the rule with the lowest precedence number wins. For a single-rule lab, precedence 1 is fine.

GE verification checklist

  • Splash template displays (not basic accept page)
  • Registration form accepts input
  • Welcome / access-granted page shows after Accept
  • Client gets 10.10.0.x (VLAN 10)
  • GE Monitor → Dashboard: Total Users = 1
  • GE → Configure → Users: guest email appears in list
  • Guest can browse internet (google.com loads)

If splash page doesn't change to GE template

1. Confirm "Enable ExtremeGuest Essentials" toggle is ON in the XIQ network policy SSID config
2. Confirm policy was pushed to AP1 (green audit icon in XIQ Manage → Devices)
3. Confirm Onboarding Rule exists with correct SSID name (must be exact match including case)
4. Try: Manage → Devices → AP1 → clear AP cache or reboot

Sprint 2 — AirDefense Essentials

AirDefense Essentials is XIQ's cloud-based WIDS/WIPS (Wireless Intrusion Detection/Prevention System). Your AP3000 acts as a sensor — it scans RF channels while still serving clients (Radio-Share mode) and reports threats to the AirDefense cloud engine. You see results in the AirDefense dashboard inside XIQ.

What AirDefense detects

CategoryThreat types
ExploitsImpersonation attacks, rogue exploits, DoS deauth floods, DoS disassociation, TKIP out-of-sequence, DTP trunk negotiation
ReconnaissanceWireless scanning, probing, NetBIOS traffic, unauthorized sniffing
Rogue ActivityRogue APs (detected by BSS MAC range analysis), rogue clients, evil twins
VulnerabilitiesWeak encryption (WEP leakage), misconfigured APs

AP3000 sensor mode

AP3000 operates in Radio-Share mode — both radios continue to serve clients AND scan for threats on the channels they're operating on. A dedicated sensor AP (monitor-only, no client service) sees more channels but AP3000 in Radio-Share is sufficient for a lab.

Handling a rogue AP alarm

  1. Click the alarm row → Alarm Details dialog opens
  2. Review: Raised Against (target MAC), Reported By (AP1 sensor), Category
  3. If it's a known device (your own AP): click Rogue Terminate → Sanction → device added to sanctioned list, alarm clears
  4. If it's unknown: click Rogue Locate → map shows estimated position (requires ExtremeLocation Essentials)
  5. To dismiss: click Clear Alarm (skull icon)

Alarm Management — enable/disable alarm categories

AirDefense app → Settings → Alarm Management

Toggle entire alarm categories on or off. For a home lab, consider disabling Reconnaissance alarms temporarily — neighbor devices generate many INFO-level recon alarms that are not relevant to your lab.

Step 5 — Verify AirDefense is Working
  • Sensors page: AP1 shows ONLINE with recent Last Seen timestamp
  • Sensors by Connectivity widget: 1 Connected, 0 Disconnected
  • Overview dashboard: Alarms Overview widget shows > 0 alarms within 5–10 min of AP1 coming online
  • Live Alarms table: at least one alarm row visible (even INFO severity is fine)
  • Click any alarm → Alarm Details dialog opens with full info
  • Alarm Count by Severity widget: shows distribution (most will be INFO in a home lab)

If no sensors appear after 5 minutes

1. Confirm WIPS policy has "Enable AirDefense Essentials" toggled ON
2. Confirm the network policy was pushed and the AP1 config push shows green audit icon
3. Confirm AP1 is Online in XIQ Manage → Devices
4. Try: Manage → Devices → AP1 → Reboot (not factory reset)

Sprint 3 (Future) — PPSK Self-Registration & 802.1X

Deferred sprint — do when 802.1X / NPS is available

This sprint is parked until a RADIUS/NPS server is in place. The goal: employees self-service their PPSK using their corporate email, with domain validation enforced at the authentication layer.

What was learned today — the "Return Aerohive Private PSK" toggle

EP1 has a two-SSID self-registration mechanism for PPSK. An open onboarding SSID with CWP collects the employee's email, auto-generates a unique PPSK, and delivers it by email/SMS. The employee then connects to the secure PPSK SSID using the received key.

The product gap — why this sprint is deferred

EP1 v25.9.0 product gap — domain filter not in UI

EP1 v25.9.0 has no domain allowlist for PPSK self-registration. There is no field to restrict registration to @extremenetworks.com or any other domain. Without domain filtering, any person who connects to the open onboarding SSID can register and receive a PPSK — defeating the purpose of employee-only access.

Proper solution — 802.1X + RADIUS

The correct enterprise solution for "employee self-service with domain enforcement" is 802.1X PEAP-MSCHAPv2 with a RADIUS server (Windows NPS or FreeRADIUS) backed by Active Directory:

  1. Employee connects to KhKLab_Employee (802.1X SSID)
  2. AP sends RADIUS Access-Request to NPS
  3. NPS validates username/password against AD — domain membership enforced automatically
  4. On success: RADIUS returns VLAN 5 attribute → employee lands on 10.5.0.x
  5. No PPSK needed — corporate credentials ARE the key

Reminder — when to run this sprint

Run Sprint 3 when NPS/RADIUS server is available in the lab. This was deferred from the May 11 session. The 802.1X PEAP-MSCHAPv2 arc was designed then — pick it up from the May 11 EOD.
