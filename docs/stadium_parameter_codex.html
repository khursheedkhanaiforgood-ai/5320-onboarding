§ 00 — QoE Definition

Stadium Outdoor QoERecommended definition — not yet locked by customer. We are assigning it.

At a 50,000-person outdoor venue, "the network is good" is not a sentence that can be finished with a single number. It needs a formal objective that respects the crowd, protects the payment rail, and doesn't reward one sector at the expense of another.

Objective Function — Proportional Fairness Utility
U(θ) = Σᵢ log(throughputᵢ) over all admitted bulk clients Robust form: maximize P50[U(θ)] − 0.5 · (P95[U] − P5[U]) subject to P(posLatency ≤ 10ms) ≥ 99%

θ = parameter vector (the 12 search knobs). k = spread penalty weight (default 0.5). Customer-adjustable: k, POS threshold, VIP floor.

Service Tiers — Hard Constraints

POS Terminals · VLAN 50
Latency ≤ 10ms (P99) · ≤ 3ms where rTWT ON
Loss ≤ 1%. Payment rail — breach = transaction failure. When rTWT is active, hardware-scheduled slots guarantee ≤ 3ms; the optimizer enforces this tighter bound.
VIP Suite Aggregate
≥ 25 Mbps
Premium cluster — must sustain multi-screen video + conferencing throughout match.
Teams / Video Conferencing
≥ 5 Mbps per seat
Corporate suites and press box; drives VIP SLA pressure with dedicated APs.
Bulk Clients
Proportional Fairness
log(throughput) utility over remaining capacity — no single sector starves another.
Admitted-Client Floor
Count ≥ Deployment Target
Cannot strand fans. Admitted sessions ≥ target_clients or penalty fires. Prevents the optimizer from "winning" score by kicking clients rather than improving the network.
Priority Order on Conflict
POS SLA > Fairness (log-utility) > Admitted count > Aggregate throughput > Per-client peak

Explicitly sacrificed: peak per-client throughput for strong clients. In a 72k bowl full of slow phones, fairness-or-collapse dominates raw speed.

Why Proportional Fairness for Stadium

At kickoff surge — 50,000 simultaneous sessions — PF prevents monopolization. No single AP sector can starve another sector's clients because the log utility penalizes starving the bottom decile far more than it rewards enriching the top. A linear sum (maximizing total throughput) would concentrate resources in the high-MCS front rows while the upper deck degrades. PF distributes gain across the log curve.

Customer-adjustable parameters k (spread penalty weight, default 0.5) — increase to penalize variance more aggressively at expense of mean utility. POS threshold (default 10ms P99) — tighten to 5ms for premium venues. VIP floor (default 25 Mbps) — scale with suite AV load.

Stadium Outdoor vs Indoor — Archetype Divergence

The outdoor bowl has τRMS ~200–400ns from stand reflections, higher angular spread, Rician K ~3–8, and larger cell radius. These force GI 1600ns, higher TX power, and wide channel widths. Indoor premium club (same venue, different archetype): τRMS ~50–150ns, K ~10–20, smaller cells, GI 800ns, lower TX power (10–14 dBm). The QoE objective is identical; the locked parameter values differ. This codex applies specifically to the outdoor bowl archetype.

§ 02 — The Optimizer Search Set

12 Prime InputsThe knobs the optimizer actually turns — and why.

After classifying all 60 parameters by effect type and archetype context, the optimizer's true search space collapses to twelve knobs. The remaining 48 are either frozen by the outdoor-bowl regime (LOCKED), set once to "always on" (GATE), fixed by SLA contract (SLA), or scenario inputs controlled by the experiment (DEMAND). Only SEARCH parameters have an interior optimum — a value where the QoE derivative changes sign — and thus require Monte Carlo exploration rather than a point estimate.

A parameter earns SEARCH status when neither extreme of its range is correct — and the correct interior value depends on load, geometry, or another knob's state.

Why NOT in the Search Set

Locked at archetype value — not searched
  • Guard Interval (1600ns) — τRMS frozen by outdoor-bowl propagation assignment. Shorter GI would cause ISI from stand reflections. No search needed.
  • MU-MIMO Streams (8) — monotone gain in outdoor bowl; angular spread frozen at archetype value. Maximum streams always wins. Lock to AP5060 max.
  • Client Ageout (5 min) — long-dwell regime constant. Fans sit 90+ minutes. Ghost entries after halftime are the only concern; 5 min clears them without over-rotating the table.
  • Roam Cache Age (60 min) — match-duration regime constant. Cache covers the first half; halftime triggers re-auth at most once per session.
  • All binary GATEs — monotone ON; no interior tradeoff exists. OFDMA, MU-MIMO, BSS Color, rTWT, Proxy ARP, 802.11r/k/v, WMM, PMF, AirDefense: always ON for stadium outdoor.







  
§ 03 — Key Interactions

  
Four Governing InteractionsWhy Monte Carlo is required — these are the shapes a point estimate cannot see.


  
A simple grid-search at the mean scenario (P50 load, nominal RF) misses these four interaction structures. Each one creates a region of the parameter space where the outcome distribution has high variance — the optimizer must explore via Monte Carlo to estimate P5/P50/P95 utility and confirm the POS SLA constraint holds at the tail.


  

    

      
Interaction 01

      
CWmin BE × Utilization Load Cliff

      
At u > 0.7 (stadium kickoff), CWmin=7 causes exponential collision increase — measured as +3ms mean latency spike across BE queue. The cliff location is stochastic: Pareto demand means some MC draws cross u=0.7, others don't. A point estimate at P50 load may show CWmin=7 as safe; P95 load reveals POS SLA breach. The optimizer must jointly select CWmin and confirm P99 POS latency across the full load distribution, not just at mean occupancy.

    

    

      
Interaction 02

      
TXOP BE × rTWT Scheduled Slots

      
rTWT assigns POS and IoT terminals to known delivery windows on the channel schedule. When TXOP_BE is long (3008 μs), bulk data flows from fan devices compete for those scheduled windows — POS latency rises even though the POS devices themselves have priority. Interaction is only active when rTWT=ON (always ON for stadium). Optimizer must jointly resolve TXOP_BE and CWmin to protect the rTWT schedule while maintaining bulk throughput.

    

    

      
Interaction 03

      
Channel Width × TX Power — The SINR Ridge

      
160 MHz halves SINR per subcarrier vs 80 MHz at the same TX power — edge seats may drop one MCS tier. At higher TX power (20+ dBm), 160 MHz recovers and outperforms. The bw5g × txPwr interaction surface has a ridge: below a TX power threshold, 80 MHz wins; above it, 160 MHz wins. The ridge location depends on sector spacing (inter-AP distance), which varies by stadium tier. Cannot optimize channel width and TX power independently.

    

    

      
Interaction 04

      
MCS Floor × Max Clients × Min RSSI — Admission Policy

      
Three-way coupling in the admission decision. Tightening MCS Floor excludes slow clients but reduces the remaining population's airtime competition. Lowering Min RSSI admits edge-seat fans but increases the fraction at low MCS. Raising Max Clients admits more devices but increases association table pressure. Each knob independently changes which clients are admitted; the admitted set's throughput distribution shifts — and the log-utility objective is sensitive to the bottom decile of that distribution. Cannot optimize any one admission knob in isolation.

    

  


  

    Simulator limitation
    The current stadium_wifi_simulator_v2.html models these interactions as multiplicative scalar adjustments — it cannot derive the interior optima described above. It is a faithful teaching instrument but a pre-alpha tuning engine. These four interaction structures are the primary justification for the Monte Carlo + optimizer architecture described in Journey to the Digital Twin.

    Codex applicability
    Every GATE parameter listed in §01 remains ON for the indoor archetype. The search set (§02) applies with recalibrated priors. The primary changes are: locked values for GI, TX power, channel width, and client ageout shift to shorter/lower values; the MU-MIMO stream lock drops from 8 to 4 subject to measured angular spread. The four interaction structures (§03) remain valid — their ridge locations shift with the propagation parameters.
