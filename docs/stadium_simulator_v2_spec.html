Three input tiers: Toggle parameters apply a multiplicative correction factor to the base airtime model. Slider parameters reshape the capacity curve continuously. Toggles — 4 Multiplicative Correction Factors

Each toggle is ON/OFF in EP1. When ON, it applies a multiplier to the base airtime calculation in the simulator.

Toggle 1
OFDMA
Default: OFF — all 6 ax/be profiles
Stadium: ON
Simulator multiplier: DL ×1.35 / UL ×1.20. Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access — lets one AP serve multiple clients per TXOP by splitting subcarriers. Most impactful single toggle for dense uplink (fan photo/video uploads).
Toggle 2
BSS Coloring
Default: OFF — all 6 ax/be profiles
Stadium: ON
Simulator multiplier: co-channel interference ×0.80 (20% reduction). Each AP marks frames with a colour ID — overlapping-cell clients suppress CCA deferral on other-colour transmissions. Critical for Lumen Field where 100+ APs share the same channels.
Toggle 3
MU-MIMO (DL + UL)
Default: DL ON (5G only) / UL ON (5G only)
Stadium: ON all bands (verify AP SKU)
Simulator multiplier: DL ×4 / UL ×2 spatial streams (AP5060). Multi-User MIMO — AP transmits to multiple clients simultaneously via beamforming. be-5G/be-6G support listed for AP5020/AP4020/AP4020X only — confirm AP5060 SKU support before enabling.
Toggle 4
TWT (Target Wake Time)
Default: OFF — all 6 ax/be profiles
Stadium: ON (be profiles, POS/IoT SSIDs)
Simulator effect: removes POS/IoT contention from shared airtime pool. AP schedules wake windows for POS terminals and IoT sensors — they only contend for the channel at their assigned slot. Frees unscheduled airtime for fan traffic.

Sliders — 8 Continuous Parameters

These reshape the capacity curve. Each becomes a range slider in the simulator UI, with a live recalculation on every drag.

Slider 1
Max TX Power (dBm)
Default: 20 dBm (2.4G) · 23 dBm (5G/6G)
Stadium: 17–20 dBm on 5/6 GHz
Drives cell radius → RSSI contour → spatial reuse count. Reducing TX power shrinks cells, enables more simultaneous non-interfering transmissions. Primary lever for per-AP client density. Range: 3–30 dBm.
Slider 2
Min TX Power / TPC Floor (dBm)
Default: 3 dBm
Stadium: 5–8 dBm
Prevents TPC from collapsing the cell below minimum coverage. Sets the guaranteed coverage floor in the simulator's link budget calculation. Range: 3–20 dBm.
Slider 3
SNR Threshold (dB)
Default: 15 dB (ax/be) · 20 dB (ac)
Stadium: 25 dB
Admission control boundary — clients below this SNR are rejected. Drives effective cell size and average MCS index in the simulator. Raising from 15 → 25 dB forces earlier roaming, raises average MCS, increases per-client throughput at the cost of tighter coverage. Range: 10–35 dB.
Slider 4
Max Clients per Radio
Default: 100
Stadium: 50–75
Hard capacity ceiling. When reached, AP rejects new associations — forces load-based roaming. Primary input to per-AP load calculation and system-wide capacity ceiling. Range: 10–200.
Slider 5
Channel Width (MHz)
Default: 80 MHz (5G) · 160 MHz (6G)
Stadium: 160 MHz (5G) · 320 MHz (6G, WiFi 7)
Input to the raw PHY rate lookup table (MCS × NSS × BW). 160 MHz doubles PHY rate vs 80 MHz. 320 MHz doubles again — WiFi 7 only on be-6G. Wider channels also mean fewer non-overlapping channels available; simulator must account for channel reuse penalty.
Slider 6
Band Steering Mode
Default: 80/20 Balanced (80% to 2.4 GHz)
Stadium: Encourage 5 GHz
Controls 2.4G / 5G / 6G client distribution ratio — the three independent airtime pools in the multi-band model. 80/20 Balanced is catastrophic for capacity: it steers 80% of clients to the lowest-capacity band. Encourage 5GHz inverts that ratio.
Slider 7
Airtime per Client Threshold (%)
Default: 0 (disabled)
Stadium: 5–10%
Airtime fairness enforcement. When enabled, no single client can consume more than this % of total radio airtime — prevents legacy 802.11g clients from hogging shared airtime. Adjusts per-client floor vs ceiling model in the simulator. Range: 0–50%.
Slider 8
Anchor Period (s)
Default: 60 s
Stadium: 30 s
Roaming aggressiveness — how long a client can stay associated before being steered. Shorter anchor = tighter RSSI distribution across the cell = higher average MCS index. Feeds the MCS distribution curve in the simulator. Range: 5–300 s.

Constants — 4 Efficiency Multipliers

These are identical across all 7 profiles (or have one recommended setting). Load them from the profile at simulator startup — change only when swapping AP hardware.

#ParameterValue (all profiles)Simulator EffectAction
C1 Short Guard Interval (GI) ON GI efficiency multiplier ×1.111 vs long GI (~11% throughput gain). Indoor stadium RF has low delay spread — short GI is always valid. Keep ON. Fixed constant in model.
C2 A-MPDU Aggregation ON Aggregation efficiency factor — reduces MAC overhead by 60–80%. Single most impactful PHY efficiency parameter for bulk data. Critical at stadium density. Keep ON. Fixed constant in model.
C3 Beacon Period (TUs) 100 TUs = 102.4 ms Management frame overhead as % of airtime. 100 TUs ≈ 0.5–1% overhead at stadium density — acceptable. Reducing to 50 TUs doubles overhead. Keep at default. Keep at 100 TUs. Fixed constant in model.
C4 WMM TXOP per AC (µs) VO=1504 · VI=3008 · BE=0 · BK=0 Per-service-class channel hold time — differentiates POS / VIP / Teams / Guest throughput model. Voice TXOP (1504 µs) protects Teams/Comms SSID. Raise VO for stadium comms if Teams traffic degrades. Load from profile. Raise VO on Teams SSID.

8. Optional Settings — SSID-Level Parameters

These are SSID-level settings from the EP1 Optional Settings panel, separate from the radio profile. They govern legacy rate compatibility, security policy, roaming assistance, QoS enforcement, multicast handling, and client timers. All defaults shown as found in EP1.

8.1 — Radio and Rates (Legacy Mode Compatibility)

Controls which legacy 802.11 protocol generations are allowed to associate. Disabling older modes raises the minimum data rate floor and frees airtime currently consumed by legacy beacon/management frames.

BandModes AvailableEP1 DefaultStadium TargetRationale
2.4 GHz802.11b / 802.11gBoth enabledDisable 802.11b802.11b devices lock the AP into 1–11 Mbps protection mode for all clients. Zero 802.11b devices expected at Lumen Field.
5 GHz / 6 GHz802.11aEnabledDisable 802.11a802.11a is 20 MHz / 54 Mbps max. No modern device needs it — enabling it only adds OFDM legacy preamble overhead.
2.4 GHz / 5 GHz802.11n / 802.11ac / 802.11axAll enabledAll enabledKeep all HT/VHT/HE generations. Disabling 802.11n would orphan older iPhones/Android still common in fan crowds.

Simulator impact: Disabling 802.11b removes legacy protection overhead — models as airtime efficiency +8–15% on 2.4 GHz. Add a "Legacy Mode" toggle to v2 that enables/disables 802.11b/g and shows the airtime delta.

8.5 — 802.11k / 802.11v (Roaming Assistance)

These two are mandatory ON for any stadium deployment. They are the software layer that makes physical roaming work at Lumen Field density. 802.11k + 802.11v together reduce roaming time from ~500 ms (blind scan) to <50 ms (assisted transition).

ProtocolEP1 DefaultStadium TargetWhat It DoesSimulator Impact
802.11k — Neighbor ReportsOffOnAP sends client a ranked list of nearby APs (RSSI, channel, load). Client uses the list to pre-scan roam candidates instead of scanning all channels blind. Eliminates the 200–400 ms blind scan phase.Roaming latency model: blind scan 400 ms → assisted 50 ms. Feeds the degradation level timeline at kick-off when 72K devices associate simultaneously.
802.11v — BSS Transition MgmtOffOnAP can proactively request a client to roam to a less-loaded AP (BSS Transition Request). Enables load-based steering from the network side — not just client-side RSSI triggering. Works with the Max Clients/Radio slider.When a radio hits Max Clients ceiling, 802.11v triggers BTM Request to steer excess clients. Without 802.11v, new clients are simply rejected — no graceful overflow. Feeds load-balancing model.

8.7 — Broadcast & Multicast Handling

At 72,000 fans, broadcast and multicast storms are a primary capacity killer. These settings are critical.

SettingEP1 DefaultStadium TargetSimulator Impact
Convert IP Multicast to UnicastAutoAlwaysMulticast frames sent at the lowest common MCS (often BPSK/QPSK) to reach all clients — at 72K devices this consumes enormous airtime. Converting to unicast lets each frame use the client's negotiated MCS. Airtime savings: 60–80% on multicast-heavy traffic.
Channel Utilisation Threshold6040Triggers multicast-to-unicast conversion when channel utilisation exceeds this %. Lower threshold = earlier conversion. At stadium density, channel is often above 60% — set 40 to ensure conversion kicks in during build-up phase, not just peak.
Membership Count Threshold1010 (keep)Min group members before multicast-to-unicast triggers. 10 is appropriate — below this, native multicast is fine.
Non-Essential Broadcast FilteringOffOnDrops non-essential broadcast frames (NetBIOS, SSDP, mDNS floods) before they hit the air. At 72K clients, each broadcast is replicated to every client — filtering eliminates the tail of control-plane noise. Feeds broadcast overhead constant in simulator.
Enable Multicast DropOffSelectiveDrops all multicast if enabled. Too aggressive for stadium (breaks mDNS needed for AirPlay/Chromecast on VIP). Use Non-Essential Broadcast Filtering instead. Only enable Multicast Drop on POS SSID where multicast is never needed.
